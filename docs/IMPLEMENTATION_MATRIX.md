Those endpoints exist for the biometric device (they carry a `BiometricEventId`) and a check-in posts a daily wage automatically; a desktop button would manufacture attendance the device never saw and post real wage money for it. Correction - permissioned, audited, reconciling the financial delta - is the legitimate manager action and is what the screen is built around.
    - **Backup** exposes create and verify, never restore. Restore is the one command that can erase committed financial history; the backend guards it with a mandatory reason, a literal confirmation phrase, a zero-active-operations check and a manager approval, and putting it one tap from "create backup" would undermine all four by making it look like a peer action.
    - **Menu** shows prices but does not edit them. A price change rewrites what every future order is charged and has its own authorization path (`menu_manage` / `offers_manage`); product availability is the genuinely operational, reversible, per-shift action, so that is the only write offered.
    - **Recipes** withholds food-cost % rather than estimating it whenever the server reports an incomplete ingredient cost - "unknown cost is not zero" applied to the UI, not just the domain.
    - **Payroll** offers only the single transition that is legal from the run's current status, mirroring the server's own transition table instead of presenting steps that would be rejected.

31. ~~**Product photos**~~ — **DONE** (2026-09-10): `ProductImageEntity` (migration `AddProductImages`), keyed on the product id, in its own table because every menu read loads products and almost none want the bytes; the bytes live in the database so a restore brings the pictures back with the menu. `ProductImageRules` (domain) caps at 512KB and accepts JPEG/PNG/WebP verified **against the file's own header** — every till downloads every menu photo and holds them for the length of a shift, and a card is ~250px wide; SVG is refused because it is a script host, and the WebP check reads the `WEBP` tag not just the `RIFF` container, which also wraps WAV and AVI. The menu response carries a per-product version stamp, never bytes; clients build `/api/products/{id}/image?v={stamp}`, so a replaced photo is a different URL and no cache can serve a stale one. `ImageUriToSourceConverter` holds one `BitmapImage` per URL decoded at 320px — the catalogue rebuilds its tiles on every keystroke in the search box, and without the cache the cards visibly blank and refill while somebody types. A product with no photo falls back to its **category's icon**, which says something true about the item where a placeholder would say nothing; both occupy the same fixed 104px band so a half-photographed menu still lays out as one even grid. `SetProductImageHandler`/`RemoveProductImageHandler` are gated on `menu_manage` and audited, but deliberately never enter `RetainedRequest` — that exists so an ambiguous **write** can be replayed verbatim, and half a megabyte of image bytes has no business in a durable retry file. Both clients render photos identically; they show the same menu, and a picture on one till but not the other would read as a fault. 10 new `ProductImageRulesTests`.

32. ~~**Best sellers, computed**~~ — **DONE** (2026-09-10): the agreed replacement for a manual favourite star. `MenuResponse.BestSellerProductIds` carries the products actually sold most over the last 14 business days, ranked, capped at 12, filtered to products still on the menu. Fourteen days survives a quiet Tuesday and still moves when the menu or the season does; a lifetime ranking would freeze whatever sold most in the restaurant's first year. There is deliberately **no** hand-set flag anywhere: a flag someone set once stops being true and nobody notices. The shortcut is its own chip at the head of the category strip and is **not** added to `Categories` — putting it there would make every one of its products appear twice in a search, and would give it a share of its own in anything that walks the category list. A restaurant with no sales history gets no chip rather than an empty one. **Not yet mirrored in the Call Center client** — see the open items below.

33. ~~**Supplier due dates and payables aging**~~ — **DONE** (2026-09-10): `PurchaseEntity.DueDate` (migration `AddPurchaseDueDate`) is nullable and stays null unless a term was actually agreed; neither the receive form nor the editor prefills one, because a prefilled due date becomes an agreed one nobody agreed to, which is exactly how a payables report starts inventing overdue invoices. `PayablesAging` (domain) keeps two questions apart: "how old is this invoice" is always answerable from its receipt date, while "is it overdue" is only answerable when a due date exists — an invoice without one is not overdue and is not on time either, and gets its own bucket, with the unscheduled total stated separately on both the purchasing screen and the owner dashboard. Buckets are the ordinary accounting ones and are a way of reading the numbers, not a policy about when to pay. `SetPurchaseDueDateHandler` exists because terms are frequently agreed after the goods arrive; it moves no money so it carries none of receiving's treasury machinery, and it is audited because it changes what the report calls overdue. `GET /api/purchases/payables-aging` is registered **before** `/api/purchases/{id}` so the literal segment is not swallowed by the id route. The invoice list is built from each purchase's own remaining balance rather than the supplier ledger: the ledger is the source of truth for what a *supplier* is owed but does not attribute payments to individual invoices, so it cannot say which invoice is late; the two agree because every command that changes a remaining balance recomputes it from the ledger at the moment of the write. 9 new `PayablesAgingTests` + `PayablesAgingVerticalSliceTests` (written, **not yet run**).

Only the file survives the restore (the `BackupRecords` row for it is written to the database about to be overwritten), so the safety file's path is also recorded in the audit entry for the restore itself. **Not covered by an automated test, for the same reason item 27's restore sequence is not**: reaching it requires passing every gate, and the next statement forces the shared SQL Server instance into single-user mode and kills every concurrently running test's connection.

## Acceptance audit and its remediation (2026-09-10)

The system was run end to end against the live server and the real database - roughly seventy HTTP
calls across cashier, delivery, inventory, purchasing, treasury, payroll and reporting - rather than
reviewed on paper. The full report is in `docs/FINAL_VERIFICATION_PROGRESS.md`. Verdict at the time:
**ready with blockers**. Everything below was then fixed and re-verified the same way.

36. ~~**Blocker: orders never reached the kitchen**~~ — **DONE** (2026-09-10): no print job was created
    at checkout by anything. `CheckoutOrderHandler` contained no reference to `PrintJob`, and in the
    cashier client the only call site for `CreatePrintJobsAsync` was the manual reprint button in
    Order Lookup. Worse than the absence: `AutoPrintReceipt`/`AutoPrintPreparationTicket` existed in
    the database, defaulted to **true**, were editable in the Settings screen, and were read by no
    code that decides to print - the screen promised something that did not happen. Proven by placing
    order `#0150` (a Charcoal item) and reading back `print-jobs: []`.
    `OrderAutoPrintService` now creates the tickets **server-side**, after the sale commits and
    outside its transaction, because a kitchen ticket must not depend on the client surviving the
    moment after payment. It never throws - a printer, a queue or a settings read must never roll
    back money already taken - and its idempotency key is derived from the checkout's own, so a
    retried checkout returns the same tickets instead of printing the kitchen a second copy. The
    Call Center's own client-side print call is removed: two independent implementations of "print
    the order" is exactly how one of them came to be missing. Verified live - one order with a
    Charcoal item produced `Charcoal` + `Receipt`, and replaying the same checkout still produced
    exactly two. The restaurant's four printers map onto the four existing `PrintRoute` values
    (Receipt / CallCenter / Charcoal / Grill), one `Falah.PrintAgent` instance each, and each kitchen
    ticket already carries only that station's own items.
    Because a printing failure must never fail the sale, `CheckoutOrderResult` now carries
    `PrintJobsQueued` and both stations show a warning when tickets could not be queued - otherwise
    a ticket that never reached the kitchen looks exactly like one that did.

37. ~~**Blocker: no global exception handler**~~ — **DONE** (2026-09-10): `Program.cs` had no
    `UseExceptionHandler`, so any unhandled failure in any of 171 endpoints reached the caller as a
    bare 500. `/api/approvals/issue` did exactly that for a missing `action` - the value travelled to
    the database and failed on a NOT NULL constraint - on the one path that authorises comps, refunds
    and permission overrides. Confirmed in both environments: Development leaked the table and column
    name, Production returned 500 with an empty body. The action is now validated in the domain
    (`AuthorizationPolicy.IssueManagerApproval`), and the handler returns an Arabic message plus a
    reference while the detail goes to the log.

38. ~~**Eight further defects from the same audit**~~ — **DONE** (2026-09-10):
    - **Split payment legs were invisible.** Persisted in `SplitPaymentsJson` and used to attribute
      each channel's total, but absent from `OrderDetailsDto` - so an order reading "multi" could not
      be reconciled from any screen, receipt or report. Now exposed.
    - **The wage ratio reported 0% with no wages posted at all** - the same "unknown is not zero"
      defect already fixed for food cost, sitting in the same card. `ComputeOperatingMargin` now takes
      a posting count and returns null when nothing was recorded; zero stays zero when entries exist
      and summed to it.
    - **An unknown treasury account answered 200 with a balance of zero** instead of 404.
    - **A mistyped purchase unit cost silently rewrote the restaurant's margin.** This system values
      an ingredient at its LAST purchase price, so one line becomes the cost basis immediately. Proven
      by accident during the audit: receiving at 1,500 instead of 15,000 moved a recipe from 2,250 to
      225 and the day's food cost from 6,750 to 675, with nothing recording that it had happened.
      `PurchasingRules.DescribeUnitCostChange` now describes the move and `ReceivePurchaseHandler`
      audits it with previous, new and factor, flagged `warning` at a tenfold change. Deliberately
      **no block and no threshold policy**: prices move, pack sizes change, and what counts as a
      reasonable cost is the restaurant's call. The dashboard surfaces the count.
    - **Nothing said a business day had been open for seven days** - every daily figure and every
      attendance record was attaching to a date that had ended a week earlier.
    - **Lateness was measured from when the open shift began**, so a shift left open across days made
      everyone days late: a normal check-in recorded 6,377 minutes and posted an automatic wage
      deduction (capped at two penalty hours, so 100 EGP of real money). It now requires a same-day
      baseline and records no lateness without one, rather than a number it cannot stand behind.
    - **`DriverOperationalStatus` reached clients as a bare integer**, so a supervisor screen showed
      "0". The label now travels with the value.
    - **Recipe profitability required the caller to already know the selling price.** It now resolves
      the product's own price when none is given.

39. ~~**Two owner-visibility gaps the audit named**~~ — **DONE** (2026-09-10): the four payment-channel
    figures never summed to "collected" and nothing explained the difference. A settled COD order IS
    collected, but its money arrives through a driver settlement rather than through any sale's
    payment leg, so it appears in none of the four. `SettledFromDriversPiastres` now carries that
    figure and the sales-split card names it. And **payroll**, the one module the audit could not
    judge because it had never been run end to end, was run for real - which surfaced a refusal that

    Payroll itself is verified working on the live system: base 600,000 less deductions 10,000 gives
