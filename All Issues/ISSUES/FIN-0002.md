## 4. Relevant historical evidence

Failing test written but never executed: tests/Falah.Server.IntegrationTests/SplitOrderVoidTests.cs:90.

## 5. Expected behaviour

Voiding a split order reverses (or flags for return) every leg already collected, so drawer expected cash matches reality.

## 6. Actual code-supported behaviour

The cash movement stays posted while the order is cancelled; the drawer expects cash for a sale that no longer exists.

## 7. Root cause

Cancellation was designed for single-method pending electronic orders; split legs were never modelled.

## 8. Business impact

Shift close shows an overage (or hides a cash theft); sales and treasury disagree.

## 9. Reproduction / failure scenario

100 ج cash + 200 ج InstaPay; transfer never arrives; cashier cancels the pending payment → order cancelled, 100 ج remains in drawer expected cash.

## 10. Existing safeguards

Stock disposition and audit entry on cancel.

## 11. Missing safeguards

Reversal movement of posted legs; refund path for split orders.

## 12. Recommended remediation

In the same transaction, post a reversing movement for each posted leg, dated at the cancellation; whether cash is handed back automatically is an owner refund-policy decision.

## 13. Alternative approaches

Forbid cancelling the electronic leg of a split order; require a manager void of the whole order that reverses all legs.

## 14. Required regression tests

Run SplitOrderVoidTests (expect RED first); then shift-close and treasury classes.
